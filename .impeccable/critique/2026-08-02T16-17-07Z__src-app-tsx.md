## Overall Impression

The interface already has a strong point of view and excellent spatial discipline. The single biggest opportunity is to make operational truth as carefully designed as the chrome: failure recovery, zero-scan loading, and long-session legibility currently fall below the quality of the visual system.

## What's Working

1. **The spatial contract is excellent.** Top changes radar context, left changes the application, and bottom controls time. The menu overlays the map without moving the playback instrument.
2. **The identity is specific without becoming decorative noise.** Stormlight color stays on bounded seams and focus; persistent surfaces remain matte and restrained.
3. **The semantic foundation is strong.** Native buttons and range input, accessible names, named regions, focus entry, Escape dismissal, focus restoration, forced-colors focus rules, and reduced-motion rules are already present.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Actionable failure recovery is visually hidden

**Why it matters:** `src/App.tsx` constructs specific instructions for initialization, map, renderer, playback, unavailable-site, and retry failures, including whether the last completed scan remains displayed. It then renders that guidance only in an `sr-only` alert. A sighted operator sees `RADAR ERROR`, `RETRYING KTLX`, or `KTLX UNAVAILABLE` but not what happened or what to do.

**Fix:** Add a compact, persistent visual recovery line within the playback/status instrument or a bounded status treatment immediately above it. Preserve the terse headline, then expose one plain-language action and the last-painted preservation truth. Do not use a toast that disappears.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden src/App.tsx`

### [P2] The zero-scan state masquerades as an active paused timeline

**Why it matters:** With no observations, the UI simultaneously shows `0 / 0`, `PAUSED`, `WAITING FOR RADAR`, a saturated scrub track/thumb, and `CLICK TO INSPECT`, while transport and timeline are disabled. These facts are individually true or mechanically derived but collectively imply that a usable loop and inspectable radar already exist.

**Fix:** Give zero-scan a dedicated presentation. Suppress the inspection prompt and active-looking timeline until the first GPU paint, replace `PAUSED` with a waiting/painting state, and present one primary sentence such as `WAITING FOR KTLX RADAR` with a subordinate automatic-loading explanation.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify src/ui/radarChromeModel.ts`

### [P2] Critical operational readouts are too small for long monitoring sessions

**Why it matters:** Timeline metadata is 9px; freshness, context labels, and dBZ are largely 10px with generous letter spacing. These are the values Mistr asks the operator to trust repeatedly, and fine type becomes tiring on high-density displays or for low-vision users even when contrast technically passes.

**Fix:** Keep stencil and micro-label typography for identity and rare cue headings. Raise freshness, playback position, and dBZ to a calm 11-12px floor, reduce tracking on operational copy, and let numeric values carry more hierarchy than decorative labels.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset src/styles.css`

### [P2] The menu trigger reports the wrong relationship when the context-site panel is open

**Why it matters:** The trigger label becomes `Close Mistr menu` for any open panel, even when the separate context-site panel is open; its `aria-expanded` remains false while clicking it closes that other panel. This is a small but concrete mismatch between accessible language, visible state, and behavior.

**Fix:** Treat the menu and context triggers as separate origins. When the context-site panel is open, the menu trigger should either say/open `Mistr menu` while atomically switching panels, or remain an explicit close-all action with accurate naming and control relationship.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden src/ui/RadarChrome.tsx`

### [P3] The menu and context bar nearly fuse at the supported minimum width

**Why it matters:** At 1024x640, the 310px menu ends at x=330 while the centered context bar begins at approximately x=330.26. Nothing overlaps, but two separate instruments appear to touch, weakening the intended spatial zoning.

**Fix:** At the minimum-width breakpoint, trim the menu width or compact the context bar slightly so a deliberate 8-12px gap remains. Preserve the stable map and playback geometry.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt src/styles.css`

## Cognitive Load

The populated operating surface is low-load: choices are grouped, only one panel opens, and no decision point presents more than four actions. The **zero-scan state is moderate-load**, failing single-focus and visual-hierarchy checks because six simultaneous cues compete to describe whether radar is usable. There are no decision points with more than four visible options; the problem is competing status information, not too many actions.

## Emotional Journey

1. **Arrival -- confidence:** the sparse night surface feels deliberate and premium.
2. **Orientation -- competence:** site, product, elevation, and time use the operator's language.
3. **Waiting -- uncertainty:** disabled transport, `PAUSED`, `WAITING`, and `CLICK TO INSPECT` conflict.
4. **Operation -- flow:** fixed instrument locations and direct scrubbing support muscle memory.
5. **Failure -- avoidable anxiety:** terse visible errors with hidden recovery instructions make the operator guess whether valid radar remains.
6. **Menu -- reassurance:** short grouped overlays feel contained and reversible.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex, the power user:** Direct scrub and keyboard panel handling are good, but shortcuts are undiscoverable. The active-looking empty timeline wastes a glance, and failure labels require interpretation instead of immediately stating the recovery action.

**Sam, the accessibility-dependent user:** Native semantics, focus return, forced colors, and screen-reader recovery copy are strengths. Low-vision use is weakened by 9-10px operational type. The menu trigger's label/control mismatch while the context panel is open can also produce a confusing screen-reader sequence.

**Morgan, the experienced storm operator:** The instrument language and stable zones fit the workflow. The red flags are precisely the trust surfaces: the zero-scan state overclaims readiness, detailed failure preservation/retry truth is not visible, and freshness/dBZ typography is too fine for hours of repeated scanning.

## Minor Observations

- The About panel is visually successful but does not provide concise task help for scrubbing, recentering, inspection, or interpreting freshness.
- At 1024x640, the menu panel remains above the playback bar and the playback bar does not move; this acceptance behavior passed.
- No horizontal overflow or child-readout overlap appeared at 1440x900, 1100x700, or 1024x640.
- The earlier 640px clipping observation is not a product defect: the Tauri shell enforces a 1024x640 minimum window.
- Pointer-based dBZ interrogation is consistent with the current product contract. Keyboard site selection, map navigation, and transport remain the explicit Alpha accessibility requirements; keyboard reticle placement would be a future enhancement unless that contract changes.

## Questions to Consider

1. Should the first visible recovery line prioritize **what remains safe** (`Last completed scan remains displayed`) or **what to do next** (`Mistr is retrying automatically`)? The strongest treatment can carry both, but one should lead.
2. Should zero-scan preserve the physical outline of the playback instrument as a quiet skeleton, or should the timeline area collapse into a single loading/status field until the first paint?
3. Does task help belong as four concise rows inside About, or as small contextual hints that disappear after the operator has used each control once?

## Remediation Outcome

All blocking findings were closed in the UI/live-site hardening change:

- actionable failure and retained-display guidance is visible above the playback instrument;
- the zero-scan surface is a dedicated preparation state rather than an inactive-looking timeline;
- operational readouts use an 11px floor with tighter tracking;
- the menu and context-site triggers now maintain independent expanded/close truth; and
- compact chrome and menu geometry preserve deliberate separation at the supported minimum window.

Owner review also drove the smaller reticle and chrome, removal of stencil typography and the menu accent rail, one canonical site picker, removal of inert Product/Elevation segments, and hiding of engineering alignment points. Those changes are recorded in `docs/23_ALPHA_UI_AND_SITE_HARDENING.md` and were validated in the packaged Windows readiness matrix.
