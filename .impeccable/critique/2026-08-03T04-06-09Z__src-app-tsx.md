## Design Specificity Verdict

**Overall: 7/10. Map-only: 4/10. Radar-on: 8/10.**

Without radar, the surface resembles a generic inverted GIS blueprint: black ground, a nearly white route mesh, and small uppercase labels. Its character comes from contrast extremity rather than a uniquely Mistr visual hierarchy. With radar painted, the product becomes specific and convincing because the measured reflectivity is clearly the operational subject.

The user's eye-strain reaction is supported by both reviews and the current visual evidence. The last pass solved map disappearance, but it overshot from quiet context into persistent high-frequency contrast.

**Deterministic scan:** the Impeccable detector returned `[]`: zero findings, zero rules, and no false positives for `src/App.tsx`. This is expected because the problem is not a generic code anti-pattern. It is a perceptual hierarchy problem created by valid style layers and their runtime order.

**Visual evidence:** a fresh 1280×720 browser view showed the basemap alone, and the packaged KOKX/KTLX captures showed the same style with radar. The style contains 48 layers; 40 render above radar, including 22 line layers. Declared composites place the coastline around 6.10:1 contrast against the base, the regional motorway around 4.73:1, the state boundary around 3.87:1, and the regional major road around 3.35:1. No reliable user-visible detector overlay was created because mutable injection was unavailable and the detector had no findings.

## Overall Impression

The radar finally reads cleanly, but the map now behaves like a luminous wireframe. On its own, road and boundary linework becomes the subject. With radar, those same lines fracture the storm into bright filaments. The single biggest opportunity is to replace equal-opportunity brightness with a strict operational hierarchy: storm first, location second, detail on demand.

## What's Working

1. **Radar color is authoritative.** The operational reflectivity ramp feels meteorological rather than decorative.
2. **The weak-return change fixed the major defect.** The broad pale negative-dBZ disk is gone while coherent positive precipitation remains.
3. **Neutral geography is semantically correct.** Roads and labels do not borrow warning colors, and their dark halos preserve readability over precipitation.

## Cognitive Load

Interaction load is low, but perceptual load is high. Four checklist failures are present:

- **Visual noise floor:** routes, state boundaries, and coastlines sit in a narrow pale luminance band.
- **Hierarchy compression:** users must inspect topology to distinguish roads, political boundaries, shorelines, and storm-crossing context.
- **Multi-task demand:** radar-on requires separating precipitation, weak-return texture, road mesh, labels, and boundaries simultaneously.
- **Poor progressive disclosure:** the initial regional view exposes more road detail than is necessary to orient to one radar site.

## Priority Issues

### P1 — The regional basemap is a white wireframe on black

**Why it matters:** At the initial radar-coverage scale, the map becomes the first read. Continuous bright linework creates halation, fatigue, and an old technical-blueprint feeling instead of a calm night instrument.

**Evidence:** The current background is `rgb(12,12,12)`, while coastline, regional motorway, regional major roads, and state boundaries use the same near-white RGB at opacities from 0.38 through 0.56.

**Fix:** Establish three tiers. Important cities and the selected geographic anchor are primary; coastline, state boundaries, and interstate/trunk roads are secondary; other roads wait for closer zoom. As a starting experiment, test coastline around 0.34–0.42 opacity, state boundaries around 0.22–0.28, and regional motorways around 0.28–0.34. Use fewer thinner strokes and avoid revealing primary/secondary route density until roughly zoom 8.5.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable quieter`

### P1 — Above-radar context visibly splinters the storm

**Why it matters:** Roads remain readable, but pale casings and dark minor details cut precipitation into a mesh. The user sees route geometry before storm structure in dense metros.

**Evidence:** Radar is inserted before the first symbol layer. Forty style layers render above it, including coastline, buildings, roads, highway labels, boundaries, and place labels. Runtime order, rather than depth, determines the result.

**Fix:** Split the context graph deliberately. Put paths, minor/service roads, railways, buildings, and secondary labels below radar. Keep only coastline, state boundaries, interstate/trunk, major cities, and essential highway identifiers above radar. Above-radar roads should be recognizable on inspection, not equal in salience to precipitation.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable quieter`

### P2 — Weak positive returns still produce a shimmering static field

**Why it matters:** Fine blue/cyan texture remains granular around KTLX. During playback, that texture will feel busier than a still screenshot and adds to long-session fatigue.

**Evidence:** The display ramp currently reaches about 38% opacity at 5 dBZ, 65% at 10 dBZ, and 85% at 15 dBZ before becoming fully opaque at 20 dBZ.

**Fix:** Keep exact interrogation and full opacity at 20+ dBZ, but evaluate a calmer presentation ramp near 15–25% at 5 dBZ, 40–50% at 10 dBZ, and 65–75% at 15 dBZ. Validate against genuine weak drizzle and snow before adopting it. Do not lower global radar opacity or call this clutter classification.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable quieter`

### P2 — Labels are legible but not comfortably hierarchical

**Why it matters:** Small uppercase labels are trapped inside the road lattice. Over radar, the halo preserves letters but not fast place recognition.

**Fix:** Show fewer labels with more authority: important cities at 13–15px medium-weight title case; quieter state/region context; secondary towns and highway labels at 10–11px and later zoom. Preserve the near-black halo without increasing label count.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

## Persona Red Flags

### Alex — impatient power user

Pan, zoom, and recenter are immediate, but route structure repeatedly captures the eye. Alex cannot isolate a precipitation core at a glance and will perceive the visual decoding cost as slowness even when rendering performance is excellent.

### Sam — accessibility-dependent or low-vision user

White hairlines on near-black can produce halation rather than comfort. Small uppercase labels require magnification, and partially transparent blue/cyan weak returns are difficult to separate from texture. High nominal contrast does not compensate for excessive line density.

### Morgan — long-session storm monitor

Morgan needs major roads and cities for storm positioning, but not every corridor at equal brightness. During hours of playback, persistent route crossings and weak-return shimmer create cumulative fatigue. The current map is compelling in a still screenshot but too insistent for a long watch session.

## Minor Observations

- Fixed north-up, zero-pitch presentation is excellent for operational orientation.
- Water is already tonally distinct, so the bright outline around every visible water polygon adds more lace than needed.
- The high-contrast attribution block creates another small glare point in the upper-right.
- Once negative returns disappear, the radar origin and coverage are less obvious; a future subtle origin/coverage cue may be more useful than tertiary roads.
- The browser emitted a missing `circle-11` sprite warning. Place text remained visible, so this is not the source of the eye-strain problem.

## Questions to Consider

1. Has “context must remain legible” accidentally become “context must remain bright everywhere”?
2. At initial regional scale, does the user need anything beyond coastline, state boundaries, major cities, and interstate/trunk routes?
3. Are we optimizing for an impressive validation screenshot or for comfortable eyes after three continuous hours?
4. Should map context remain static, or briefly strengthen during pan/zoom and settle quieter afterward?
5. Is a subtle radar-origin or coverage cue more valuable than dozens of tertiary route lines?
